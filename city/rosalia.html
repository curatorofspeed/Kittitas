Our Town

Rolling hills, small-town roots

Located just south of Spokane, Rosalia sits inside Whitman County — the nation's largest wheat-producing county — where the Palouse hills run green in spring and turn to golden grain at harvest.

Highway 195 puts Spokane, Pullman, and Cheney within an easy 45-minute drive for work, universities, sports, and shopping. Updated Ziply fiber Wi-Fi also makes working from home a real option, and a small airport four miles west offers tie-down space and budget-friendly hangar sites for private pilots.

Housing runs from early-1900s homes to new construction, and the community is family-friendly with preschool-through-grade-12 education. For leisure there's a city park and play area, tennis and basketball courts, three churches, a library, a small business district, and an outstanding fire and ambulance service. Come for a visit — or stay awhile and discover the joy of a friendly rural community.

Dog Licensing

Dogs within city limits must be licensed each year: $10 fixed, $20 unfixed. Purchase by the last day of February to avoid a $20 late fee.

License at City Hall

Recycling Center

Single-stream recycling — all accepted items go in one bin. Open during the day, Monday–Friday. Please don't leave items outside; dumping is illegal.

Things to Do

Explore the Palouse

History, wide-open views, and outdoor recreation — all within easy reach of downtown Rosalia.

State Heritage Site

Steptoe Battlefield

A four-acre day-use park marking the 1858 battle between U.S. Army forces and area tribes.

Washington State Parks State Park

Steptoe Butte

A quartzite bluff high above the Palouse with unmatched 360° views of the rolling hills.

Washington State Parks City Park

Rosalia City Park

Shaded lawns, a covered picnic area, a rentable kitchen, kids' play area, and tent camping by reservation.

View on the map Local History

Perkins House & Cabin

Whitman County Historical Society's landmark home and pioneer cabin, open for the curious.

Historical Society
Trail

Palouse to Cascades Trail

The cross-state rail-trail (formerly the John Wayne Pioneer Trail) for hikers, cyclists, and riders. Tent camping is allowed at the city park.

Landmark

Historic Texaco & EV Charging

Charge your electric vehicle near the historic Texaco service station, then explore eateries, an antique shop, a bookstore, and Granny's Thrift.
