Our Story

A Town Built on Coal

Welcome to the City of Roslyn! Located 80 miles east of Seattle near Interstate 90 in sunny central Washington, Roslyn played an important role in Washington State history. The extensive coal fields in the area fueled the Northern Pacific Railroad's trains during construction and early operation of a direct rail line through the Cascade Mountains.

Men from coal mining regions in the United States, Europe, and elsewhere came to work in the mines. English, Italian, and Slavic immigrants were among the early settlers, and a significant proportion of the town's early residents were foreign born. In 1888, the Northern Pacific Coal Company recruited more than 300 African-American miners from Virginia, North Carolina, and Kentucky, who came with their families to work in the mines.

At one time, 24 ethnic groups and nationalities were living in Roslyn. Today, many of the original families continue to make Roslyn their home.

Roslyn at a Glance

1886

Roslyn is incorporated as a coal-mining town serving the Northern Pacific Railroad.

1888

More than 300 African-American miners and their families arrive from Virginia, North Carolina, and Kentucky.

24

Ethnic groups and nationalities living in Roslyn at the town's peak — a legacy preserved in its historic cemeteries.

Today
