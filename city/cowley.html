Welcome to Cowley

A small town with a wide horizon

Nestled in picturesque Big Horn County in northwestern Wyoming, Cowley is a small but vibrant town with a rich history and striking scenery. Sitting about 25 miles north of Lovell at the foot of the Bighorn Mountains, the town looks out on wide, open country — with the mountains rising to the west and the plains stretching east.

Its position near the Shoshone River and close to both the mountains and the plains makes Cowley a welcoming spot for nature lovers and history enthusiasts alike, with easy access to Bighorn Canyon, the Pryor Mountains, Shell Canyon, and miles of hiking, fishing, and hunting country.

Community life

Though small in population, Cowley has a strong sense of community and an easy, welcoming pace. Neighbors know one another and work together to keep the town's character intact. Residents enjoy a local school, an outdoor swimming pool, parks, a top-flight lighted baseball field, and several small businesses providing essential services — with world-class outdoor recreation just beyond the town limits.

Whether you're passing through or thinking of putting down roots, Cowley is a place where Wyoming's rural charm and western heritage are alive and well.

Our History

How Cowley began

The town of Cowley was settled by a group of Mormon pioneers called to the Big Horn Basin. It was named in honor of Matthias F. Cowley, then one of the Apostles of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. In February 1900, a group of explorers was sent to find a suitable location for a colony in northern Wyoming, meeting with William F. Cody, who held a state permit to build an irrigation canal.

The first company of pioneers arrived on May 2, 1900, and began the Sidon Canal to carry water from the Shoshone River — over 30 miles long, completed in 1904. The founding of Cowley

By the winter of 1900 there were roughly 18 log houses in the area. The first school opened in a log house in January 1901, taught by Eliza R. Black, with about 24–30 students.

The families who built those first homes
Those original homes were built by Robert Baird, John Black, John Burke, Hyrum Cook, John Dickson, Robert Fraser, William Graham, David Lewis, Gilbert Marchant, Alfred Nebeker, William Partridge, William Simmons, George Taggart, Henry Tucker, Franklin Turnbow, Lemuel Willis, W. W. Willis, and George Harston.

The Big Horn Academy

On September 26, 1910, the Big Horn Academy opened in Cowley as the area's first high school. The first graduating class, in 1912, had 13 students, and the first four-year class graduated in 1915. A new stone building was erected for the Academy in 1916. In 1925 the name changed to Cowley High School, home of the Jaguars, which remained in the Academy building until declining enrollment closed the school in 1983.

From 1984, students attended the consolidated Rocky Mountain High School in Byron. For the 2010–2011 school year, Rocky Mountain High School moved to Cowley with the completion of a new high school building.

History adapted from the Town of Cowley's records. Historical details drawn primarily from With Book and Plow by Mark N. Partridge (2003), courtesy of the Cowley Pioneer Museum History Center.
