Since 1877

The stone that built the Capitol

Wilkeson grew up around coal discovered in the Carbon River canyon, and around a sandstone quarry opened in the 1880s — the oldest in the Pacific Northwest. That warm, cream-colored stone was cut here and shipped out to face the dome and exterior of the Washington State Legislative Building in Olympia, along with landmarks across the region.

Down the valley, a half-mile row of beehive coke ovens once glowed day and night. What remains is now listed on the National Register of Historic Places — a reminder of the town that helped build Washington.

Fun fact the town still enjoys: the state's 1920s bill for that Capitol sandstone was never quite settled. With interest, Wilkeson jokes it's now worth about $5 million — though we'd happily call it even for a plaque.
