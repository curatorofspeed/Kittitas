Our story

A valley with deep roots

Stanley sits on land that has drawn people for centuries, from the Shenandoah's first inhabitants to the families who call it home today.

The earliest recorded inhabitants of the Stanley area were the Shawnee, who lived throughout Page County until the outbreak of the French and Indian War in 1754. The first person other than a Native American to view the Stanley Plain was likely John Lederer, a German physician and explorer, who reached the summit of the Blue Ridge near Milam Gap around 1669 and 1670.

Forty-six years later, in 1716, Virginia's colorful deputy governor Alexander Spotswood led his well-provisioned band down into the valley, fording the South Fork of the Shenandoah River near the village of Alma, just west of Stanley. The town has grown from those frontier beginnings into the close-knit community it is today.
