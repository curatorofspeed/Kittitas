Welcome

A quiet place, steadily good

South Prairie is one of Pierce County's plateau communities, perched above the Puyallup River valley on ground forged from glacial retreat and volcanic activity. The area abounds with small-town charm, rural living, and outdoor recreation, with Mount Rainier and Lake Tapps framing the horizon.

In many places the town looks much as it did a hundred years ago. South Prairie Creek runs clear through the meadows and tall trees, making this a peaceful spot where the outside world's hectic pace feels far away.

A brief history of South Prairie

With thanks to Geanellen Kuranko for preserving the town's story.

The story of South Prairie is one of steady, quiet growth — from a single land claim in the wilderness to a railroad and coal town, to the peaceful community it remains today.

In 1854, Paul Emery filed a donation land claim and became the first permanent settler in the area, farming the fertile prairie south of Connell's and Porter's prairies — the location that would give the town its name. For years the little settlement grew slowly, tucked into the tall trees above South Prairie Creek.

Everything changed with coal. When deposits were discovered in the surrounding hills around 1875, the promise of mining drew people, capital, and — most importantly — a railroad. The Northern Pacific reached the area, and South Prairie became a junction and shipping point for coal hauled down from Wilkeson, Burnett, and the mines beyond.

Turn of the centurySteadily developing into something good

By the 1880s the town had a store, platted streets, and a growing population. Frank Bisson platted South Prairie in 1888, and the town took shape around the rail line and the creek. A post office, schools, churches, and businesses followed as families put down roots.

For a time the town was briefly renamed "Cascade Junction," but residents resisted the change. On February 17, 1909, the community was officially incorporated as the Town of South Prairie — the name it has carried proudly ever since.

A peaceful spot where the outside world's hectic pace seems but a bad dream. — On life in South Prairie

Famous folksHome of a big-league ballplayer

South Prairie's proudest sporting son is Paul Strand, who grew up in the town and went on to a professional baseball career. He pitched and played for the Boston Braves and later became one of the most prolific hitters in the minor leagues, helping win a pennant in the years around 1912–1913. For a town this small, a genuine big-league ballplayer is a point of lasting pride.

Notes of interestA town shaped by its landscape

Much of what makes South Prairie special is unchanged from a century ago. The creek still runs clear, the meadows are still ringed with tall evergreens, and Mount Rainier still stands watch to the southeast. The mines are long quiet and the railroad's heyday has passed, but the plateau, the water, and the small-town character remain — the reasons people have called this place home for well over a century.
